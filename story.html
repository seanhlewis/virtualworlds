Where people go

What people in shared virtual worlds reveal about places, intentions, and crowds, when nobody tells them where to go.

Sean Hardesty Lewis · MIT · 2026

Scroll

Every evening, people walk in. Nobody gives them a task.

Each point of light is one real person, replayed from telemetry. The building is drawn from its own geometry.

Their paths carve the same corridors.

Scroll to scrub time. Each trail fades, but what it leaves behind accumulates.

Where people stop is a property of the place.

Each column is how often a walk ends on that square metre.

Both halves draw the same map.

0.95rank correlation

Split the crowd in two, with no person in common, and both halves draw the same map.

But where they go next is nearly a guess.

The crowd's own map picks the right spot only 5% of the time.

Until they start to move.

9 → 40%in 8 seconds

Columns show the belief about where this person is heading. Intent, not the map, is the bottleneck.

So we model people as agents with goals.

When to leave. Who or where to go. Then walk. Nothing else is scripted from the recording.

The social structure survives.

Who stands alone and who stands together is reproduced, and it transfers to worlds the model has never seen.
